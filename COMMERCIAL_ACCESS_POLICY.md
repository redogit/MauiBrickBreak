`PUBLICLY_VISIBLE != COMMERCIALLY_LICENSED`

`COMMERCIAL_INQUIRY != PERMISSION`

`BUSINESS_ACCESS = DENY_UNLESS_EXPLICITLY_GRANTED`

## Consumer product boundary

This policy does **not** prevent the owner from offering RMAOS MINGX directly to individual end users under owner-defined consumer terms, including the currently declared Mini ($0.99) and Full / Pro Advanced ($1.00) product tiers.

A consumer purchase is a personal-use product transaction. It does not grant the buyer or another business a right to resell, sublicense, host, integrate, train on, monetize, or commercially exploit the underlying research, code, data, interfaces, or services unless a separate written license says so.

## Existing-license precedence

This policy does not retroactively cancel rights already granted under a valid existing license.

- Material already released under MIT remains governed by MIT for that covered material.
- Material already released under GNU GPL remains governed by the applicable GPL terms for that covered material.
- Upstream-derived and third-party material remains governed by its original license, attribution, and provenance terms.
- Source-specific rights and cultural/provenance restrictions remain attached to their sources.

When this policy conflicts with an already-applicable license for specific material, the existing license controls that material.

## Future original work

Unless a file, subproject, or release explicitly says otherwise, newly created original RMAOS MINGX material and other future original work intended to follow this policy should be treated as **all rights reserved / no commercial license granted** until the owner deliberately publishes different terms.

A later commercial authorization must identify at least:

1. the exact material or revision;
2. the permitted commercial use;
3. data/API/device access, if any;
4. security and privacy obligations;
5. redistribution or sublicensing rights, if any;
6. duration and termination conditions;
7. any payment or other consideration.

No such commercial authorization exists merely because a repository, branch, page, package, interface, or demonstration can be accessed.
